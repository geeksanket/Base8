Chapter 01 · Reconnaissance

How tall is what you cannot climb?

You are a NASA scientist guiding an autonomous robotic rover across the red dust of Mars. A sheer, vertical mountain rises from the terrain, blocking your scientific path.

Your rover's titanium wheels cannot scale the vertical rockface. Yet Mission Control requires the summit elevation before the orbital satellite passes overhead.

Mission Instrument: Rangefinder LIDAR

The rover aims its laser diode at the distant peak. We can measure the ground distance (24.0 km) and the launch angle (36.87°). How do we find the height?

Chapter 02 · Geometric Abstraction

From physical terrain to pure mathematics.

When the rover connects its laser to the mountain summit, three physical axes align: the flat Martian ground, the vertical cliff, and the line-of-sight laser path.

Together, they form an exact right-angled triangle. Mars gravity pulls 100% straight down toward the planetary core, guaranteeing that the vertical elevation meets the horizontal ground at an exact 90° right angle.

The 90° Gravity Anchor

Gravity never falls sideways. This square corner locks all triangle proportions into fixed, predictable values.

Chapter 03 · The Anatomy

Three sides. Three distinct roles.

From the rover's point of observation at angle θ, every side has a specific geometric identity:

• Adjacent (Base): 24.0 km — The measured ground distance tracked by wheel odometry.
• Opposite (Height): ? km — The vertical elevation rising straight to the summit.
• Hypotenuse (Slant): — The line-of-sight laser distance straight from rover to peak.

Because this triangle has a fixed 90° corner, the ratio between the Opposite (Height) and the Adjacent (Base) depends solely on the angle θ.

Chapter 04 · The Ratio

The Tangent Breakthrough.

In trigonometry, the function that compares the vertical rise to the horizontal run is called the Tangent.

tan(θ) = Opposite ÷ Adjacent = Height ÷ Distance
Height = Distance × tan(θ)

Think of the tangent as an elevation multiplier. Knowing our baseline distance of 24 km and an angle of 36.87° is mathematically all we need to solve the mountain!
